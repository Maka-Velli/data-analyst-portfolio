# E-commerce Business Questions (SQL)

Eight business questions about Olist, a Brazilian online marketplace, answered
with MySQL 8.0. The data covers 96,478 delivered orders and R$13.22M of revenue
from September 2016 to August 2018.

## The question

How is the marketplace performing, and where can it improve? The queries cover
revenue, product categories, month-over-month growth, delivery, customer repeat
behaviour, seller concentration and payment methods.

## The data

- **Source:** [Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/olistbr/brazilian-ecommerce)
- **Tables:** `customers`, `sellers`, `products`, `orders`, `order_items`, `order_payments`
- **Size:** 99,441 orders, 112,650 order items, 32,951 products, 3,095 sellers and 99,441 customers
- **Period:** September 2016 to August 2018
- **Currency:** Brazilian reais (R$). Revenue means item price without freight, on delivered orders only.
- **Limitations:** this version has no review scores and no cost or marketing data, so the findings show the method rather than a full business diagnosis.

## Setup

```bash
# 1. Get the data
python download_data.py                 # saves to ../.rawdata

# 2. Load it into MySQL 8.0
mysql --local-infile=1 -u root -p < schema.sql

# 3. Run the eight queries
mysql --local-infile=1 -u root -p olist < queries.sql

# 4. Regenerate and check the expected results
python verify_results.py
```

`schema.sql` creates the `olist` database, the six tables with primary and
foreign keys, loads the CSV files with `LOAD DATA LOCAL INFILE`, and prints
row-count and referential-integrity checks. All six foreign keys pass with zero
orphan rows.

`verify_results.py` transpiles the MySQL queries to DuckDB with `sqlglot`, runs
them against the same CSV files and writes `expected_results.md`. The headline
numbers are asserted, so a change in the data or the SQL fails loudly.

## The eight questions

| # | Question | SQL techniques |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How much revenue and how many delivered orders are there? | `JOIN`, `COUNT(DISTINCT)`, aggregation |
| 2 | Which product categories bring in the most revenue? | CTE, window share of total, `LIMIT` |
| 3 | How did revenue grow month over month? | CTE, `LAG()` window function, `DATE_FORMAT` |
| 4 | How does delivery performance vary by customer state? | `DATEDIFF`, conditional aggregation, `HAVING` |
| 5 | Is shipping faster and cheaper inside the same state? | CTE, `CASE`, conditional aggregation, freight share |
| 6 | How often do customers come back? | Two CTEs, bucketing, window share |
| 7 | Who are the top 10 sellers by revenue? | `RANK()`, window share of total, `GROUP BY` |
| 8 | How do customers pay? | CTE, share of value, average installments |

## Key findings

- **Fast growth, with a Black Friday spike.** Monthly revenue rose from R$111.8K
  in January 2017 to R$977.5K in May 2018. November 2017 jumped 52.4%, then
  December fell 26.5%. One day explains it: 24 November 2017 had 1,147 orders
  against a 243-order daily average for the month.
- **Revenue is spread across many categories.** The top 10 of 73 categories
  bring in 63.2% of revenue. Health and beauty is first (R$1.23M). Watches and
  gifts is second with far fewer items (5,859 against 10,953 for bed, bath and
  table) because its items cost about R$199 each against R$93.
- **Delivery depends on location.** The average delivery takes 12.6 days and
  8.1% of orders are late. São Paulo averages 8.7 days; Amazonas takes 26.4 and
  Alagoas 24.5. Alagoas has the highest late share (23.9%).
- **Same-state shipping is twice as fast.** Items shipped inside their state
  arrive in 7.9 days against 15.0 days across states, are late less often (6.0%
  against 9.0%) and cost less in freight (13.0% of the price against 18.3%).
  Only 36.2% of items travel that way.
- **Customers rarely come back.** 97.0% of customers ordered once, 2.8% twice
  and 0.2% three or more times.
- **Credit cards dominate payments.** They account for 78.5% of payment value,
  with an average of 3.5 installments. Boleto is next at 18.0%, then vouchers
  (2.2%) and debit cards (1.4%).
- **No seller dependence.** The top 10 sellers make up 13.3% of revenue and none
  is above 1.72%. Nine of the ten are in São Paulo state.
